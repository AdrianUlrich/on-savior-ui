Ostranauts Social Physics

Every social move in the game, decompiled from the shipped JSON into a working model: which chargen traits unlock or forbid which moves, what each move actually does to the thirteen psychological need axes, and how the whole conversation system hangs together — priced against the years each trait costs (or refunds) in character creation.

01 · The machine

How a conversation is computed

Under the hood a chat is bookkeeping. Everything below is read straight from StreamingAssets/data — none of it is speculation, and spot-checks match the wiki's hand-collected numbers exactly.

Needs are the currency

Each character tracks need axes — Contact, Esteem, Intimacy, Privacy, Self-Respect, Meaning… — scored 0–210 where lower is better. A rising need is a frustration building up.

Moves are transactions

A social move pushes axes on both sides. A dark joke drops the target's Contact need by 12 (satisfying) while feeding your own Meaning. An insult inflates their Esteem need. Every delta is in the data.

Traits are the gates

Most moves carry an any-of list of enabling traits and a forbid list. There are no all-required gates in the current build — and the check short-circuits on the first match, so a second enabling trait adds nothing to a move you already have.

Traits come in mirrored pairs

Gregarious↔Shy, Kind↔Cruel, Leader↔Follower… In chargen a beneficial trait costs 1–3 years of your life; taking its harmful mirror refunds years to spend elsewhere.

        07 · Method & caveats

        Where this comes from

        Parsed from the game's shipped data files (): moves from interactions/*.json, gates resolved through condtrigs, effects through the loot condition tables, year costs from traitscores.json. Debug-only moves are excluded. Direction convention verified against the wiki: need stats run 0–210, lower is better.

        Selection mechanics (decompiled from Assembly-CSharp.dll via ILSpy, CondTrigger.Triggered and GUISocialCombat2.SetData): the conversation UI builds one button per reply in the current move's aInverse list that passes its gate. A gate first rolls its flat per-move chance (shown in the tooltips here), then checks forbids, then walks the any-of trait list and returns on the first match — matching more traits changes nothing. Even the "(Trait)" tag the game shows on a button is just the first match. So with free refreshes, trait value is exactly marginal unlocks; redundancy has zero likelihood payoff.

        Caveats. Temporary mood variants of traits (IsBraveTemp) are folded into their base trait. NPC-side reply choice among valid moves and relationship formation (acquaintance/partner rolls) are not analyzed here.
